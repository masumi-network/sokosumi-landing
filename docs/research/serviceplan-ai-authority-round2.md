# Serviceplan + AI authority research, round 2

**Research date:** 2026-08-26
**Scope:** New authority-building opportunities for Sokosumi around (1) Serviceplan Group + AI and (2) AI marketing agency / marketing agency + AI search intent.
**Baseline reviewed first:** `docs/research/serviceplan-ai-group.md` and the nine current pages in `apps/sokosumi/content/serviceplan-ai/pages.mjs`.

## Method and evidence rules

This pass deliberately looked for facts and buyer questions that the existing dossier and CMS pages do not yet cover. Primary sources were preferred: Serviceplan Group and House of Communication product pages, news releases and case pages; the European Commission's AI Act guidance; and the Serviceplan Group Business Partner Code of Conduct.

- **Verified fact** means the claim is stated on a first-party or official primary source linked beside it.
- **Vendor-reported result** means Serviceplan, Mediaplus or a group unit publishes the number; it is not treated as independently audited.
- **Inference** means a content or entity conclusion derived from the sources. It is labelled and should not be presented as a first-party fact.
- **Gap** means the reviewed public material does not answer the question.
- Search-intent recommendations below are qualitative. They require Ahrefs/Search Console validation before final prioritisation; no search volume or ranking claim is made here.

### 1.7 Governance and compliance: one new source, substantial remaining gaps

Verified:

- Serviceplan Group's March 2026 Business Partner Code of Conduct applies to its business partners and requires them to protect intellectual property and data; when they use AI, the AI-based developments must comply with data-protection and security regulations. Source: https://www.house-of-communication.com/content/dam/house-of-communication-aem/global/about-us/climate/sp-gh-kg-code-of-conduct-business-partners-march-2026.pdf.coredownload.pdf
- This is a business-partner minimum standard, **not** a comprehensive public responsible-AI policy, model-risk framework or product security document.
- EU AI Act Article 50 transparency obligations have applied since **2 August 2026**. The European Commission says providers of directly interactive systems must inform people that they are interacting with AI unless it is obvious, and providers of generative systems must support machine-readable detection of generated/manipulated content. Deployers have additional duties for emotion recognition, biometric categorisation, deepfakes and certain public-interest text. Source: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act
- The Commission explains that an advertising company remains the deployer when employees, contractors or freelancers operate the system under its authority. Source: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act

Still not public in the reviewed Serviceplan material:

- A group-wide responsible-AI or AI ethics policy.
- Product-by-product model providers, subprocessors, retention periods, security architecture, audit reports or conformity documentation.
- A public Article 50 implementation statement for Generate.AI, AI Coworkers or other Serviceplan systems.
- The governance boundary between Serviceplan Group, Plan.Net, NMKR, agent providers and clients when an agent is deployed.

Content consequence:

- A buyer guide can explain what to ask an AI marketing agency and cite official law, but it must not imply that Serviceplan or Sokosumi has satisfied every applicable obligation merely because a page says “EU AI Act compliant.” Legal scope depends on the specific system and deployment.

### High-information-gain angle

Most generic pages describe tools or promise efficiency. Sokosumi can be more useful by showing:

- where AI sits across the entire marketing value chain;
- which delivery model fits a one-off task, a custom workflow or an enterprise operating-model transformation;
- the evidence quality of each case;
- the data, approval and governance questions a buyer should ask;
- where public information ends.
